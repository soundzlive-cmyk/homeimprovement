Windows

Replacement windows and new installations that look sharp, seal tight and cut your energy bills.

Windows shape how a home looks from the curb and how it feels inside. The right ones let in more light, keep out drafts and noise, and lower the cost of heating and cooling through a New England winter.

Whether you're replacing tired, drafty windows or adding new openings in an addition or remodel, our own crew handles it start to finish: measuring, ordering, installation, flashing, insulation and trim.

New openings, done right

Window installation

New window installation is for additions, remodels and new openings, anywhere a window doesn't exist yet. We frame and size the rough opening, install the unit plumb, level and square, then wrap it with flashing tape and a sill pan so water has nowhere to go. Low-expansion foam seals the gaps, and finished interior and exterior trim make it look like it was always there.

  • Framing Header, jack studs and rough opening sized to the unit.
  • Weatherproofing Sill pan, flashing tape and house wrap lapped the right way.
  • Insulation Low-expansion foam to stop drafts without bowing the frame.
  • Finish Interior casing, exterior trim and caulk, painted or wrapped.
A window is only as good as the flashing around it.
No. 02

Out with the drafts

Window replacement

Replacement windows swap out old, failing units for energy-efficient ones. Depending on the condition of your frames, we install an insert window into the existing frame for a quick, clean upgrade with minimal disruption, or do a full-frame replacement down to the rough opening when there's rot or water damage to fix. Either way, we protect your floors, remove and haul away the old windows, and leave every opening sealed and trimmed.

  • Time to replace? Drafts, fogging between the panes, sashes that stick or won't stay up, or soft, rotted wood.
  • Insert replacement Fits inside the existing frame. Faster, keeps your interior trim.
  • Full-frame replacement Down to the studs to fix rot and reflash the opening.
  • Energy savings Double or triple-pane, Low-E glass and gas fills to cut heat loss.
Better windows mean a quieter, warmer house and a smaller heating bill.
